# Research Notes

The drafts are intended as reusable worldwide foundations for research master's admission, scholarships, and supervisor-supported applications. They are not tailored to a single institution. Current official guidance reviewed emphasizes:

- A recommender who knows the applicant well and is qualified to evaluate graduate-study potential.
- Specific, story-based evidence rather than unsupported superlatives.
- Direct discussion of academic preparation, research ability, character, and likely contribution to graduate study.
- A letter tailored to the intended field while remaining truthful to the recommender's firsthand knowledge.
- Referee-specific firsthand evidence, and alignment with the particular research proposal or program when known.
- Following each award's own reference form, confidentiality, authorship, format, and submission requirements; a generic letter may not replace a prescribed form.

The cross-program lesson is not that every scholarship uses identical letter rules. DAAD calls may require a DAAD reference form; Fulbright instructions specify project-informed, confidential recommendations and direct referee submission; Oxford's guidance asks for relevant, evidence-based assessment. Erasmus Mundus requirements are set by the particular consortium/program. Always follow the live application call, not this general draft set.

The bracketed sections are intentional. They identify places where the professor should add only details they can personally verify.
