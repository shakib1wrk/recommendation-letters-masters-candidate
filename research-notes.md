# Research Notes

The drafts were shaped by current graduate-admissions guidance emphasizing:

- A recommender who knows the applicant well and is qualified to evaluate graduate-study potential.
- Specific, story-based evidence rather than unsupported superlatives.
- Direct discussion of academic preparation, research ability, character, and likely contribution to graduate study.
- A letter tailored to the intended field while remaining truthful to the recommender’s firsthand knowledge.

Sources consulted:

1. Stanford Graduate Admissions, “Recommendations”: https://gradadmissions.stanford.edu/apply/recommendations
2. Stanford Academic Advising, “Asking for Letters of Recommendation”: https://advising.stanford.edu/current-students/advising-student-handbook/letters-recommendation
3. Stanford Humanities and Sciences, “Requesting Recommendation Letters”: https://humsci.stanford.edu/prospective-students/guide-getting-grad-school/requesting-recommendation-letters
4. MIT Admissions, “How to write good letters of recommendation”: https://mitadmissions.org/apply/parents-educators/writingrecs/

The bracketed sections are intentional. They identify places where the professor should add only details they can personally verify.
